EmptyHands at 20: two decades of discipline, growth and community

By Azeem Mushtaq Sensei & the EmptyHands team

Twenty years ago a simple idea took root in East London. It was never about building the biggest martial arts school, producing champions or creating a brand. It was about making a place where people could learn, challenge themselves and discover what martial arts can offer beyond punching and kicking.

In March 2006, Azeem Mushtaq Sensei founded EmptyHands in Ilford on the principles of Bushidō: discipline, respect, humility and purpose. Since then, hundreds of students and their families have trained, grown and found a second home at the dojo. This anniversary is a moment to look back at the people, the challenges and the milestones that made EmptyHands what it is.

Twenty years at a glance

  • 1994 - Azeem begins his formal Karate training as a founding member of an East London dojo.
  • 1999 - He launches ZenKarate, one of the first online Karate magazines, and begins studying Tai Chi Chuan with Master John Ding.
  • 2003 - He graduates from the British School of Shiatsu-Dō and founds the EmptyHands clinic.
  • 2006 - EmptyHands opens its doors in Ilford.
  • 2011 - The dojo awards its first black belt.
  • 2013 - Azeem completes an MA in Coaching at Oxford Brookes, developing the first Karate Coaching Model.
  • 2020 - The Virtual Dojo keeps students training together through lockdown.
  • 2026 - Twenty years, fifteen black belts, and classes in Karate, Tai Chi, Yoga, Shiatsu and Coaching.

The beginning

The roots of EmptyHands go back long before 2006. Inspired by a family tradition of wrestling, Azeem discovered martial arts as a child and took up kickboxing as a teenager at the Gold Steel Gym in Walthamstow. After starting formal Karate training in 1994, he trained widely, learning from respected teachers including Hirokazu Kanazawa Sōke, Chris Rowen Shihan, Yoshinobu Ohta Sensei and Nobuaki Kanazawa Kanchō, while Tai Chi Chuan and Shiatsu broadened his understanding of movement, health and human performance.

By 2006 the vision was clear: one school that would bring martial arts, wellbeing and personal growth together under one roof.

Building a community

Over twenty years, hundreds of students have stepped onto the dojo floor. Some came for self-defence; others wanted fitness, confidence, discipline, friendship or simply a new challenge. Many stayed far longer than they expected. Children grew into adults, beginners became senior grades and students became instructors, among them founding members Hamza Iftikhar and Amarah Adam, who now help teach the next generation. For many, the dojo became a second family.

The path to black belt

Among the school’s proudest achievements are the fifteen students who have earned their black belt since the first was awarded in December 2011, some of whom started training at the age of three. A black belt is not an award for perfection. It stands for commitment, resilience, patience and a willingness to keep learning long after others have stopped. Each of them is celebrated on the Yudansha wall, and each is proof that success is rarely about talent alone. It is about showing up, week after week, year after year.

A school that kept evolving

Karate remains at the heart of EmptyHands, but Tai Chi Chuan, Yoga, Shiatsu and Coaching have become part of the school too. They reflect a simple belief: martial arts are not just about fighting, they are about living well. Balance, awareness, recovery and emotional resilience all belong on the path.

Learning from great teachers

Every martial artist stands on the shoulders of those who came before. Workshops with Chris Rowen Shihan gave karateka of every grade the chance to train with a top Karate instructor and authority on Goju-Ryu Karate, who trained in Japan under Gogen Yamaguchi, and the school’s wider network has connected students with leading practitioners of Karate, Tai Chi and Shiatsu.

One relationship deserves special mention. Azeem first met the renowned Shiatsu master Rex Lassalle in 2010, and what began as treatment grew into a lasting friendship. Rex shaped Azeem’s understanding of Shiatsu and in 2024 taught his Be Easy Sōtai method at the dojo. He died in February 2025, and his teaching, generosity and friendship remain an important chapter in the EmptyHands story. Read the tribute to Rex.

Giving back

The spirit of EmptyHands has always reached beyond the dojo walls, through demonstrations, workshops, charity activities and local events. The annual EmptyHands Charity Cup has become one of the school’s most cherished traditions. Open to every grade, it combines kata, kumite and team events with fundraising for a chosen charity each year.

A test like no other

Perhaps the greatest test came in 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic closed training halls. Rather than pause, the school moved online. Through the Virtual Dojo on Microsoft Teams, students kept training, supported one another and held on to routines that had become an important part of their lives. Apart physically, the community stayed connected.

The people behind the school

No anniversary would be complete without thanking the instructors, assistants, parents, families and volunteers who helped shape EmptyHands.

Special recognition goes to Irm Mushtaq Sensei, who began training in 2006, the year the dojo opened, and is now a SanDan (third-degree black belt) and Senior Instructor. Through years of coaching, mentoring and running the dojo day to day, and as the founder of EmptyHands Yoga, she has helped countless students begin and continue their journeys. Generations of senior students have also stepped forward to lead warm-ups, guide beginners and mentor younger members. Their work often goes unnoticed, but it is the foundation the school stands on.

Lessons from twenty years

The most remarkable achievements of EmptyHands cannot be measured in belts or grades. They can be seen in the shy child who found confidence, the student who overcame anxiety, the adult who found resilience after difficult times, and the families and friendships the dojo brought together.

Looking back on two decades, Azeem Sensei reflects:

“Twenty years of running EmptyHands has taught me that a dojo is never really about the instructor, the style or even the building. It is about the students, and helping them discover what they are capable of becoming. There have been successes and setbacks, lasting friendships and, inevitably, some people lost along the way. It is impossible to please everyone, but it is always possible to act with sincerity and purpose.

The greatest lesson of all has been humility. Through teaching the principles of Bushidō, I have come to understand that true martial arts are not measured by skill alone, but by character. Integrity, respect, compassion and good conduct remain the highest achievements. In the end, belts fade and trophies gather dust, but the values we pass on to others endure. That is the true legacy of a dojo.”

Looking forward

EmptyHands enters its third decade with gratitude to everyone who has been part of the journey, and with the same aim it began with. As the first line of the Dojo Kun puts it: seek perfection of character. Whether you trained for a few weeks, a few years or the whole twenty, thank you. You are part of the EmptyHands story.

If you trained at EmptyHands at any point, we would love to hear from you: get in touch and share your memories. And if you have never stepped onto the dojo floor, there is no better time to begin. Book a trial class and start your own chapter.

Here’s to the next twenty years. Osu.
